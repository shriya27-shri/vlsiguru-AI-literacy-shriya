# Mission 5 — Can AI Be Confidently Wrong?

## 1. Prompt

I asked AI assistants to explain the bandgap of silicon at room temperature and how it changes with temperature.

## 2. AI Answers

- **Gemini:** Stated that silicon's indirect bandgap is approximately 1.12 eV at 300 K and increases to about 1.17 eV near 0 K.
- **Claude:** Confirmed that silicon's bandgap is about 1.12 eV at 300 K and that it decreases as temperature rises.

## 3. Verification Sources

- [UC Berkeley EE130 reference table](https://hkn.eecs.berkeley.edu/examfiles/ee130_sp16_mt1_sol.pdf)
- [NPTEL — Semiconductor lecture](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/122101002/Slides/Module-6/Lec-34/lec34_7.htm)
- [Kiel University — Semiconductor bandgap reference](https://www.tf.uni-kiel.de/matwis/amat/semi_en/kap_3/backbone/r3_1_1.html)

These references support a silicon bandgap of approximately 1.12 eV at 300 K.

## 4. Result

**Classification: Correct, with a qualification.**

Both AI assistants gave the accepted approximate value. Gemini's use of the word “precisely” was too strong because 1.12 eV is a rounded value. The sources I checked support the bandgap claim, but I have not independently verified every additional claim in the AI responses.

## 5. Lesson Learned

AI can provide accurate technical information, but confident wording does not guarantee precision. I should verify important claims using reliable sources and pay attention to qualifications such as “approximately,” temperature, and operating conditions.
